Engine state investigation
The upstream recurrent rollback test initially failed a strict absolute 1e-5
logit threshold. A diagnostic measured full probability distributions instead
of stopping at its first small Vulkan rounding difference. Its later dirty-restore
comparison also used a reference overwritten by a separate replay that rolled
back across multiple one-token decodes, outside the snapshots from the last decode.
Keeping the original reference makes full restore into both fresh and dirty
contexts exact on CPU. Vulkan dirty-restore KL stays below 0.00005 in those three
positions. Clearing the dirty context does not change this conclusion.

A separate forced-token test used the original pelican code, a 4096-token prefix
and 192 scored positions. It exercised ordinary single-token decoding, batches of
three, the native MTP target state with two rollback slots, and deliberately wrong
drafts with alternating acceptance/rejection. All 192 top tokens agreed in every
comparison. Mixed rejection versus ordinary decoding had mean KL 0.000006839.
This rules out a large state-corruption error in these tested paths; it does not
prove all possible contexts or guarantee semantic code quality.

Three fixed input probes at the deployed ctx 262144 matched the top token and the
top-eight token IDs from ctx 16384. Their log probabilities were not all identical
(maximum top-eight delta 0.14933 on the 5367-token prompt). Separately, the complete
webpage was byte-identical across ctx 32768 and 262144 for the fixed seed.
